# Development Process

## 1. Initial Idea
My initial concept for this assignment was a "Particle Music Canvas" where moving the mouse generated colorful visual particles and corresponding sounds. However, during early testing, I realized this interaction lacked a meaningful "response loop." The particles just appeared and disappeared; they didn't feel alive. 

I decided to pivot to a "Cyber Fishkeeping" concept. Random particles became a school of fish. This provided a much more organic interaction, as the fish could react to the cursor's presence (following), clicks (scattering), and feeding (seeking), creating a stronger connection between user action and program response.

## 2. Design & Code Changes
- **From Particles to Flocking**: I implemented the Boids algorithm (Separation, Alignment, Cohesion) to make the fish move naturally as a real school.
- **Color Palette & Gradient**: I designed a specific visual style using custom hex codes. To make the fish more dynamic, I sliced the fish body into 15 segments, applying linear interpolation to create a head-to-tail gradient. The color schemes smoothly transition every 4 seconds in a 21-second loop.
- **Background & Trails**: I experimented with fluid simulations and video backgrounds, but they caused performance issues and obscured the fish. I ultimately settled on a clean, pure black background and implemented a custom trail effect that leaves fading bubble trails behind the fish.
- **Feeding Interaction**: I added a `Food` class. When left-clicked, food particles drop, and the fish's target vector prioritizes the nearest food over the mouse cursor, triggering a feeding frenzy.
- **UI & Audio Toggle**: Redesigned the audio engine using Tone.js with a soft reverb and a filter. Added a top-right UI button to toggle music on/off, improving usability.
- **Atmospheric Elements**: Added bottom swaying plants. To prevent frame drops, I used an off-screen canvas to pre-render the plant graphics.

## 3. User Trial & Feedback
**Observation**: I asked a female friend to test the web experience. She immediately moved the mouse and smiled as the fish followed. However, she hesitated to click anything, assuming the page was just a visual animation. When she finally clicked, she was surprised by the feeding interaction and said, "I didn't know I could feed them!"

**My Improvement**: To fix this lack of clarity, I added minimal, aesthetic text instructions in the bottom-left corner (`MOVE MOUSE · GUIDE FISH`, `LEFT CLICK · FEED`, `RIGHT CLICK · SCATTER`). I also added an overlay prompt to activate the audio, which doubled as an initial "click to interact" guide.

## 4. Handling Errors & Interruptions
- **Browser Autoplay Policy**: Modern browsers block audio until user interaction. I handled this gracefully by displaying a centered "CLICK ANYWHERE TO ENABLE AUDIO" overlay. Audio nodes are only initialized after the first click.
- **Performance Crashing (Rapid Clicks & Rendering)**: During testing, rapidly clicking caused browser lag, and adding 25+ animated plants caused severe frame drops. I resolved this by:
  1. Adding a 200ms debounce to the `mousePressed` function.
  2. Limiting the `maxPolyphony` of the ambient synthesizer to 4.
  3. Rendering the swaying plants onto an off-screen canvas once, and applying a gentle sway to the whole canvas, rather than calculating Bezier curves for every plant every frame.
  4. Locking the frame rate (`frameRate(60)`) to prevent GPU/CPU overload.

## 5. AI Tools Usage
I used AI (DeepSeek) to assist with code generation, debugging, and refining the math for the visual effects.
- **Suggestions kept**: The AI suggested using `lerpColor` for the smooth transition between colors, and it helped me write the debounce logic and the off-screen canvas caching for the plants.
- **Suggestions rejected/modified**: The AI initially generated fish that were too fat and used overly bright, fluorescent colors. I rejected the initial design and iterated on it: I continuously adjusted the shape parameters to compress the fat fish body into a streamlined form. Furthermore, to achieve the exact visual aesthetic I wanted, I provided specific hex color palettes (e.g., #945B90, #CE8B8D) to override the AI's default fluorescent colors. Additionally, I rejected a complex fluid-dynamics background generated by the AI because it made the screen too chaotic.
